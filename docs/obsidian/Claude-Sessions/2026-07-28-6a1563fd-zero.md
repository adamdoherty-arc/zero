### The headline: vault retrieval could not tell signal from noise

**RET-4.** The dense side of `search()` was an unfiltered
`ORDER BY embedding <=> :emb LIMIT 40`, so it always returned 40 rows however far
away they were, and RRF then scored on **rank alone** — discarding the `cosine_sim`
the same query had just computed. Measured before the fix: every query returned a
top score of **0.016393** (= 1/(60+1)). `zzqqxx nonsense gibberish flurbular` and
`GTD weekly review` scored **identically**. `search()` could never answer "nothing
relevant" — a query about a topic absent from the vault still got a full page of
confident-looking hits.

**IDX-7.** Compounding it, **16,555 of 101,505** indexed chunks (16.3%) were
sub-20-char boilerplate. Two strings — ``**Status:** `success` `` (10,393 copies)
and ``**Status:** `failure` `` (5,283), emitted by the auto-generated loop-run
notes under `00_Meta/_agent/loops/` — collapsed ~15% of the corpus onto **two**
embedding vectors, and those vectors filled the nearest-neighbour list for almost
any query. Measured: **40/40** of the dense top-40 for both `GTD weekly review`
and `ADA trading strategy`. All twelve most-common short chunks were content-free.

Fixed at both ends: a signal-length floor at index time (measured on
markdown-stripped text, so the call is about retrievable content rather than
formatting bytes) and a cosine floor giving the dense side an abstain path.
Purged the 16,555 existing stubs and VACUUMed.

| measure | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| embedding uniqueness | 81.7% | **97.3%** |
| `GTD weekly review` top cosine | 0.525 | **0.813** |
| 4 nonsense queries | 5 confident hits each | **0 hits (abstain)** |
| 5 real queries | 5 hits | 5 hits, relevant paths |

The 0.525 → 0.813 jump is the interesting number: the stub clusters had been
physically crowding real matches out of the neighbour list.

## Key Lesson

**Two consecutive runs have now found a critic-harness defect by pointing the
critic at the run's own work, and both are the same shape: the harness silently
degrades the artifact the judge is asked to rule on.**

Last run it was a 12k diff truncation that showed 3 of 7 files and manufactured a
false REJECT. This run, review 43 scored the acceptance-criteria question **0**
with *"The Acceptance Criteria section is explicitly empty"* — on a submission
carrying 11 verified ACs. `_resolve_ac` read only `ac_override` and
`sprint_description`; the endpoint accepts `runtime_evidence`, the natural name
for exactly that payload, and dropped it on the floor. The judge visibly wavered
(*"Wait, looking closer at the prompt structure…"*) before landing on ACCEPT — a
coin-flip a stricter model loses.

`runtime_evidence` is now a first-class AC source, and an empty AC set tells the
judge it is **empty, not failed**. Round 2 came back ACCEPT **5/5** with `ac=1`,
verifying the fix through the critic itself.

The generalisable rule: *a judge handed a degraded artifact produces a confident
wrong verdict, so every degradation must be made explicit in the prompt.*

Second lesson, narrower but sharp: **pgvector's HNSW graph keeps deleted rows
until VACUUM.** Immediately after the 16,555-row purge, dense queries silently
returned *fewer* than `LIMIT` rows — 0–1 instead of 5 — which reads exactly like
a broken query. Always VACUUM after a bulk delete before trusting a recall
measurement.
