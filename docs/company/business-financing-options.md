# ADA AI LLC Business Financing Options

This is an internal research kit, not legal, tax, accounting, or lending advice.
Adam must personally approve any loan, line of credit, or card application, sign
any personal guarantee, and confirm all rates, terms, and eligibility directly
with the lender before applying. Rates and program rules change frequently —
**re-verify every number against the lender at application time.**

## Operating Stance

- Entity: ADA AI LLC, Florida single-member LLC, first year of operation.
- Goal of this doc: understand the realistic financing ladder for a brand-new,
  pre-revenue (or early-revenue) single-member LLC, and what it takes to qualify.
- Honest baseline: in year one with limited business revenue and no business
  credit history, most "business" financing still rests on **Adam's personal
  credit and a personal guarantee**. Plan around that, don't fight it.
- Borrow against a **use of funds** (specific, ROI-positive spend), never to
  cover ongoing burn. Decide the amount and purpose before shopping.
- Keep business and personal cleanly separated (the finance-setup-kit stance):
  borrow into the ADA account, spend from the ADA account, document everything.

## The realistic ladder (easiest → hardest for a new LLC)

1. **Business credit card** — most accessible day one; underwrites mostly on
   Adam's personal credit. Best for software/AI subscriptions, hardware, and
   smoothing monthly spend. Almost always requires a personal guarantee.
2. **Business line of credit (LOC)** — revolving, draw-as-needed; good for
   uneven cash flow. New businesses often need 6–24 months in business and some
   revenue; online/fintech lenders are more lenient than banks (at higher cost).
3. **Term loan** — lump sum, fixed schedule; good for a one-time, defined
   purchase (equipment, build-out). Bank term loans for a brand-new LLC are hard
   without revenue/collateral; online lenders fill the gap at higher APR.
4. **SBA loan (7(a) / Microloan / 504)** — government-guaranteed, lowest cost,
   longest terms, but the most paperwork and slowest. **7(a)** is the flexible
   general-purpose program; **Microloan** (≤ $50k, via nonprofit intermediaries)
   is the most startup-friendly SBA path; **504** is for real estate/major
   equipment only.

## Recommended next step for ADA (now)

1. **Don't take a loan yet.** With limited year-one revenue, debt should wait
   for a concrete, ROI-positive use of funds. Define that first.
2. **Open a business credit card** as the foundational instrument (separate
   task, approval-gated). Before applying, document each issuer's answers using
   `finance-setup-kit.md` Step 3 (personal guarantee? personal hard inquiry?
   reports to personal bureaus? annual fee / APR / autopay). Prioritize
   minimizing personal-credit impact over rewards.
3. **Build business credit history** for 6–12 months (card paid in full, vendor
   accounts, clean books) so a LOC or SBA loan is realistic later.
4. **If financing is needed sooner**, use **SBA Lender Match**
   (https://www.sba.gov/funding-programs/loans/lender-match) and ask about the
   **Microloan** program — the most new-business-friendly SBA path.
5. **Assemble the lender-readiness packet** in parallel (separate task): EIN
   letter, Articles, operating agreement, bank statements, P&L, personal
   financial statement, use-of-funds. Having it ready shortens any future
   application.
